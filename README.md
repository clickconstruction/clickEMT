# Incident Map

Two views of one incident, for floods and other large-scale emergencies:

- **Base** (`index.html`): the map at the base or forward operating base. Operations draws the incident boundary and the zones, sets each zone's priority, assigns units, and watches units move, photos arrive (with the direction each one faces) and zones turn green as the field marks them clear. The activity log is an ICS 214 in all but name.
- **Field** (`field/`): the phone app a unit installs. It shares the unit's location, takes photos tagged with the compass heading, marks addresses and the zone clear with the search marking the team paints, reports hazards, and has one red button for "need help now". Reports are kept on the phone until there is signal.
- **People**: every responder is a record, checked in by name with the unit from the phone, with a rank or role, an agency and qualifications (swift water, boat, rope, EMT, paramedic, K9, hazmat, drone), a buddy, and the time in and out; nothing is deleted. The unit's head count follows its people. The base has a People board (everyone by unit, with status, hours on scene, a find box and qualification chips, where Operations adds, moves, checks out or removes a person), the names are on the resources board, the briefing, the 203 and every 204, and the check-in list prints as the ICS 211.
- **People sheltered and moved**: one record per person or family (who, how many, oxygen, insulin, a wheelchair, an infant, pets, from where, where now), so nobody is lost once they leave the house. A crew's house record has a row per person it takes out (who, their condition, where they went), and a family sent to a shelter shows at the top of that shelter's desk as arriving. A unit of kind Shelter desk opens on People here: the shelter's count against its beds, They're here to sign a crew's arrival in (adding the name), Sign in a person with only who required, who is here now newest first, and Find a person across every shelter and hospital; "Not listed?" sends Operations "Looking for …" with the desk's number. A record is moved (to a hospital, another shelter), left with family or by own transport, never removed, and each arrival and move is a line on the 214. On the base the shelter's card has its beds, Expected as a typed line beside the live count of crew records on their way, the needs, People here with Where now on each row, Sign in a person and Print the list; Find a person in the header says where anyone is now and since when; the situation report counts people sheltered, moved to hospital and expected, and the records with no name yet. Names never appear in anything public.

- **The 214's water words as 45m** (v79): the one duration the database writes itself, the 214's line when a crew comes out of the water, reads the way every other duration has since v76: "Unit 2 out of the water after 45m", "after 1h 5m", "after 2h" (it read "after 45 min"). - **The centre by address** (v78): an Address box on the New incident sheet, above the map centre. A few letters typed and Google offers the places that match, nearest the newest incident's centre (or the two boxes, once Use my location has filled them); one press sets latitude and longitude, reads the centre back under them, and fills Base with the place's name when Base is empty; Enter with nothing picked looks the typed words up. The words go to Google, which is fine for an incident's centre and is why a person's address never will. The key is the browser key in `config.js`, restricted to this site, with Places API (New) enabled beside Map Tiles (the Geocoding API refuses a key restricted to a site, so every call is Places); without it the box is one muted line and the sheet is as before. Incident settings' Test button says whether Places answers too. Nothing new is stored: the centre is the two numbers, as before.
- **Incident kinds and starter periods** (v77): the New incident sheet asks the kind in two rows of buttons, both optional: Natural hazard, Human-caused, Medical and public health, Search and rescue or Planned event, then its usual sub-kinds (Flood, Wildfire, Storm, Winter storm · Structure fire, Hazmat, Collapse, Crash, Utility failure · Mass casualty, Outbreak · Swift water, Wilderness, Urban · Parade, Festival, Game). A sub-kind gives period 1 a starter, written on Create as Operations' own lines so the 214 logs each: the three standing objectives (Nobody gets hurt, crews or residents; Keep it from getting worse; Protect property once people are safe) and three to five for the kind, the safety message, the water rotation and the hours on scene, what to bring; the Period sheet opens first and says "From the Flood starter. Edit any line; × removes an objective", with Place first chips for the facility kinds still to place (a press, then a click on the map) and the unit kinds to expect. A kind alone gives the standing three. × on an objective hides it from every list and every later period, never deletes it, and writes one note. The kind is said on the incident's card, the picker and under the phone's briefing title, and changes in Incident settings. Flood is written in full, the other sixteen are thin. No migration: the kind, the starter and the removed objectives live in the incident's settings until a column exists.

- **Sign in by a code, no password** (v73): the base's landing form and the phone's sign-in sheet take an email address or a mobile number, **Send code**, and the six-digit code that comes back (the email carries a link that signs you in by itself as well); six digits typed check by themselves, and a phone offers the text message's code above the keyboard. No password anywhere: not at sign-in, not for a new account (a new address or number makes its account on its first code), not after an invitation (the link signs the person in as before; the "choose a password" step after it is gone). Under the code box, **Resend** sends another code (the first stops working) and **Use another address** goes back to the box. A mobile number is read as +1 and ten digits, or kept with the country code it was written with; anything else is refused with the reason before a code is sent. The picker and the check-in screen name the account by its address or its number, and a phone signed in with a number has it filled in on Identify yourself. Accounts made before v73 keep working: the password stays in Supabase unused, and a code to the same address signs them in. Text messages need an SMS provider on the Supabase project and the email's code needs `{{ .Token }}` in the Magic Link template ([docs/handoff.md](docs/handoff.md) says what to set); until then the email's link signs in on its own. - **Since you last looked** (v45): one line above the activity log, "Since you last looked (21:10): 2 hazards, 1 emergency (1 stood down), 1 missing person (1 found), 3 PAR answers, 4 messages, 6 photos · 2 notable", with Show them (the log filtered to those lines, the notable ones first) and Seen (marks now); the mark is kept per device, so a relief or the IC coming back after an hour reads what changed in one line.
- **Small words and buttons** (v44): the Hours table reads qualifications as words; the licence scanner's refusal says "type the licence number"; "Nobody hurt, no equipment damaged" is ticked by default on the check-out list; the picker's closed row has Reopen beside Clear now; Make a unit asks the name in a sheet of the app's own; the unit panel puts where the unit is and its last report above the kind chips; the phone's menu and briefing name the Deputy Incident Commander; a water crew's Nearby says "No boat ramp on the map yet" when none is placed; a street search in Find a person asks for "someone from" there; a shelter desk's quick chips are its own (Nearly full, Need cots and blankets, Need a nurse, Bus has arrived); the 207 warning's "add a deputy" opens the Period sheet; the water row is one button until the crew goes in.
- **Paper that leaves the base** (v43): the IAP's first page ends with "Prepared by (Planning Section Chief): … · signature" and "Approved by (Incident Commander): … · signature · date and time"; every report and briefing has **Send by email** beside Copy as text (the mail app opens with the text in the body; the app sends nothing); the join card tells Safety (the period's safety message) and Bring (a new line on the Period sheet, "What to bring", carried into the next period) apart, and Getting there names the facility with its notes and the coordinates "for a satnav".
- **The phone's sheets** (v42): a sheet's Cancel and Save stay on screen however long it is (the house record's "Searched, save" no longer needs a scroll); a division reads "Assigned, not accepted · Unit 2 · 0 of 9" until the unit presses Accept on its phone (now a line in the 214, "Brush 12 accepted the assignment to B-2") or sends a report from it, and only then "In progress"; a unit with no division of its own sees "No assignment yet … stay where Operations put you" with Take photo, Report hazard, Message Operations and Need help now, not another unit's division card; its briefing no longer tells a bridge post or a desk to wait at staging, says "you alone" for a count of one, and a desk's Work area names its shelter.
- **A hazard that says how urgent it is, and the open hazards where Safety looks** (v41): the phone's Report hazard chooses nothing for the crew (Send waits for a kind), offers the message's four urgency words first, and has kinds for a person or car in the water and a tree down across the road; an act-on-now hazard sits on the base's bar ("HAZARD · Person or car in the water · B-2 · 22:12 · SO 14") with Acknowledge and Show on map until Operations acknowledges it, the log says "(act on now)" and "Hazard acknowledged by Operations"; the home panel has a Hazards open section (kind, words, division, when, who, Resolved, Show on map) and the header line counts them; a toast on the phone sits under the header while a sheet is open, never over its buttons.
- **A period that has ended says so, and times in one form** (v40): past its planned end an open period reads "OP 1 · 18:47 to 19:00 · overdue 3 h" on the base's header and the phone's menu, every report says "the planned end passed 3 h ago; the next period has not been started", the phone's briefing says "stay on until Operations starts the next period", and the Period sheet says so with Start period lit. Every day-and-time on the base and on paper (prepared, opened, joined, taken, the clearing date) reads "17 Oct 2026 21:58", never the browser's locale with seconds.
- **The newcomer's door** (v39): a phone that arrives with a join code shows, on its sign-in screen, "Code CEDAR7 is ready. Sign in below with your email or mobile number: a six-digit code comes back, no password, and only this incident's Operations sees who you are" (the words since v73; one box and Send code); the code's sentence sits on the check-in form, not in a toast; "Your unit" says a callsign or, for someone on their own, their own name will do; a **Volunteer** unit kind; and the people rows stay folded until the count is more than one, a name is typed or Add a person is pressed.
- **One head count at a time, and a missing person who is found** (v38): Start PAR closes the PAR before it (the old answers stay; the log says "Earlier PAR closed by the new one, still waiting on Unit 2"), so the base's bar and every phone show one head count; a person reported missing is marked **Found** by the unit that reported them (on the alert screen, and on the home screen after the stand-down) or by Operations on the MISSING line, which clears the line and the red row and writes a notable line ("Patel found, reported by Unit 2"). A stand-down alone never clears a missing report.
- **Accountability by name**: when a unit has its people on record, the phone's PAR card lists them as the reminder to look and answers for all of them with one tap ("All 3 accounted for"); Someone's missing opens the list to tick, then Send stores who is missing with the count, the emergency alert names them, the base's yellow bar reads "MISSING · Okafor (Unit 1) · 14:05", that person's row on the People board and the unit panel turns red with the unit's last reported position, and the 214 line names them. A unit with no names keeps the − / + count. The head count is never unknown: the check-in form asks "How many with you?" beside the names (it starts at 1, you, since v51; typing names raises it, it cannot go below them, with no names the number stands), the people beyond the ones named are kept as the unnamed count, and a unit that gave neither reads "head count unknown" in orange on the resources board and the unit panel until its PAR card takes the number; the People board nudges "Name them for the PAR". Edit on a People board row (and on the phone's People on the unit) opens the add form filled in to correct a name, rank or role, agency, qualifications or notes, and the edit is logged.
- **Time in the water, incident settings and the public set**: a boat or swift-water crew taps In the water on its home screen or menu and Out when it is done, through the outbox; the period carries a rotation (45 minutes unless Safety sets it on the Period sheet, 0 for off, carried into the next period, printed on the 204 and the 208), past which the phone shows a slim nudge to swap the crew (never a takeover) and asks again each rotation; the base shows the time on the resources rows and the unit panel, orange past the rotation and "probably left running" past three; the briefing and the situation report list who is in the water; a check-out ends the clock; every in and out is a line with the minutes. Settings, from the header: the Google Maps browser key with a Test button (the Layers panel reads it from the incident, not from a file), how many days personal records are kept after the close (30, 60 or 90), the default rotation; they carry into the next incident the same account opens, and the log says a key was set, never the key. The public set: on a cleared photo Operations writes the public caption and presses Checked (no face, no plate, no house number); Download the public set saves the checked photos only, each named by time and place with its caption inside, captions.txt with the time, the place to the street and the caption (never coordinates or the unit's note) and the cleared-but-unchecked photos listed as missing; Public information and the situation report count cleared and checked.
- **Members and roles, and personal records that clear by rule**: the six-letter join code joins field units only; each incident has an Operations code of its own, shown on the Operations home panel with Copy and New code (never printed, never on the join card), and whoever joins with it is Operations. The People board's Members tab lists everyone with their role, agency and identity state (verified, licence on file, name and phone only), where Operations makes a member Operations or a unit, removes one (archived, never deleted: what they sent stays, their phone says they were removed and offers Sign out, what it queued before the removal still lands) and puts them back; the person who opened the incident is always Operations. Thirty days after an incident closes its personal records clear by rule: the licence numbers, dates of birth, addresses and licence photos of members who are in no open incident, and the names of the people sheltered (their counts and needs stay). The closed incident's card shows the date with Clear now and Keep 30 more days, the clearing runs nightly and on the base's next open, and one line goes in the 214. This is the one deletion in the app, for personal data only, never for work product.
- **The command staff, the organization chart and the transfer of command (ICS-100)**: each period seats the Public Information Officer (the cleared photos go to them) and the Liaison Officer beside the IC and Safety, with one agency representative and phone per agency on scene for the Liaison, drawn from the units and printed on the 203. The organization chart (ICS 207) is drawn from the seats, the divisions and their supervisors, the units and their people, with the span of control counted on every box; past seven it turns orange and the briefing says "Operations is at 1 : 8", and a branch word on the divisions groups them on the 207, the 203 and the 204. The period also seats a Deputy Incident Commander (v31), ICS-100's other remedy for a span past seven: named on the period sheet beside the IC, carried into the next period, printed on the 203 and drawn under the IC on the 207, whose warning points at the seat ("add a deputy") until it is filled. A different name in the Incident Commander's seat asks whether command is being transferred: the transfer tells every unit with a message each acknowledges, opens the briefing for the handover, and is a notable line on the 214; who held command when is kept and printed on the 203. Every unit has a kind (engine, boat crew, swift-water team, high-water vehicle, ambulance, search, K9, drone, pump), set at check-in, grouped on the resources board, read on the 203 and 204, and matched to a request ("On scene now: Unit 2, boat crew on B-1, 1.4 mi").
- **Briefings on the phone, from the data**: after check-in a unit reads its own briefing, ICS-100's items assembled from the incident as it is (situation, your job, who you work with, work area, radio, supplies, shift, safety and PPE), kept in the menu and current as Operations changes things. "If someone is hurt" answers the IAP's fourth question for that unit: the medic on the unit from its qualifications or the nearest one, the emergency channel, the nearest hospital with distance and directions, an ambulance a tap away, and the check-out reminder, orange where something is missing; the same lines print on each 204 under Medical (ICS 206). The join card is the deployment briefing: the incident, where to check in, the assignment, when to report, how to get there, the radio and what to bring, drawn from the data around the code.
- **Planning, Logistics and Finance**: each period seats all five section chiefs beside the IC and Safety, carried from the last period, printed on the 203 and read at the briefing; an empty seat says "not set". The field asks for what it needs from its inbox (hands, a boat, an ambulance, fuel, equipment, supplies) with how many, by when in the same four words as messages, and where; Operations works each request on its home panel from asked to approved, ordered and filled, or says it cannot be filled, with a word back that the unit sees, and the situation report's "Resources needed" is written from the open ones (ICS 213RR). Checking out walks the unit through the demobilization list; nothing is required, and what is not ticked goes to Operations with the check-out, on the unit's panel and the 211. The Hours page gives Finance every person and unit with the time in, out and the hours, for a period or the whole incident, as a page or a CSV; a row counted to now or to the period's end because nobody pressed check out is marked.
- **Agencies, public information and replay**: the incident names its lead agency and each period its unified command, and every report says who is in charge and who is on scene from where. Operations clears a photo for the Public Information Officer one at a time; nothing is public by default. "Download cleared photos" saves the cleared set as one zip, each picture named by its time and division with its caption (what, where, when, whose; never coordinates) written into the file, where Photos and newsroom tools read it, and listed in captions.txt. Every position a unit reports is kept, and the base's Replay plays the period, the last hour or the whole incident back on a slider, with the photos appearing as they were taken.
- **The map underneath, and your own imagery over it**: a Layers panel with the map sources on one side (streets from OpenStreetMap, satellite from Esri, Google Maps streets and satellite when a key is set) and the overlays on the other: a picture Operations adds right there, such as a fresh aerial photo, which lands on the map and is dragged into place by its corners (v67), laid over the map for everyone, with show, opacity and Adjust a click away. The legend's status entries light the divisions in that status.
- **A right-click card for a division**: its rank among the divisions, its priority (changeable right there), the unit, progress, hazards, photos and last activity, from the map or the list.
- **Identify yourself**: before a unit can check in, the account says who it is once: name and phone, and a driver's licence read from the barcode on its back (name, licence number, expiry, address filled in) or photographed on the front. A volunteer with no licence checks in with a name and a phone alone; the base marks the unit "identity not given" (its row, its panel, the 211) for Operations to check in person. Operations sees it on the unit's panel and marks it verified; another unit never sees it.
- **Join by QR**: the base shows the join code as a QR beside it and prints a join card; a unit's phone shows the same code from its menu; a phone scans one at check-in, or follows the link in it, and lands on the field app with the code filled in.
- **Light and dark**: a dark mode for the overnight shift, chosen on each screen or following the system, with the map dimmed to match.
- **The activity log**: folds to its latest line or opens, filtered by where a line came from, searched, the hours marked, every line linked to the photo, division, structure or facility it is about, new lines counted while you look elsewhere, and printed whole as the ICS 214. Operations stars the lines that matter: the Notable chip keeps them, and the printed 214 leads with them. Pop out puts the log alone in its own window for a second screen, live, with a line's link opening the thing on the main screen.
- **Messages with urgency and a spot**: a unit says how soon its message needs acting on, one of three steps on a scale (now, before dark, tomorrow), flags it stuck when its work stops until the thing is sorted, and sends the spot it is about by default, dragging the pin to the exact place on a small map; the base shows the badges and a bubble on the map.
- **Reports**: a situation report (ICS 209) assembled from the data with the words Operations adds, and the Incident Action Plan stitched from the 202, 203, every division's 204 with its map, the 205, 206 and 208; each printable or copied as text.
- **Facilities and the communications plan**: the command post, staging areas, camps, shelters, points of distribution, hospitals, boat ramps and helibases placed on the map, each with a contact; the plan of which channel is for what (ICS 205). The phone shows the nearest staging, boat ramp, shelter and hospital with directions. A facility card's Close puts the card away; Mark closed closes the facility itself.
- **Primary and secondary search, with a record per structure**: a zone goes not started, in progress, primary search done, cleared, in two shades of green. Every address the field clears becomes a record: occupants, people needing help, water depth, damage class, hazards, the marking painted. Operations can place the structures it knows about in advance; the field taps them on its map.
- **The Division assignment (ICS 204)**: each division carries its work assignment in words, special instructions, radio channel and supervisor; the base prints the 204 for the truck with a map of the division (its outline over OpenStreetMap, the structures by address, hazards, facilities and the way to the ones off the map), and the phone shows the words on its card.
- **Operational periods**: the incident runs in numbered periods with their command names, safety message and objectives (ICS 202); a zone says which objective it serves. Starting the next period carries open objectives forward. The shift-change briefing assembles the whole picture from the data, printable or copied as text.
- **Accountability**: every unit carries a resource status as ICS names it, assigned, available, out of service or checked out, set by its assignments and by the unit or Operations, with its head count and agency. The base shows the resources board. A PAR asks every unit for a head count with one tap; a missing report raises an alert.
- **Messages** both ways, the ICS 213: Operations writes to one unit, the unit in a zone or everyone; each recipient acknowledges with one tap (Copy, Will do, Cannot). A safety message takes over every phone it is sent to until acknowledged, and stays on the base's yellow bar until the last one answers. Units can message Operations with quick chips or in their own words. ROADMAP.md lists what comes next.

The vocabulary follows the Incident Command System: zones are divisions, priorities are the operational period's objectives, assignments are the ICS 204, the log is the ICS 214.

A guide for each persona, with their jobs step by step and the words on the screen, is in [docs/guides/](docs/guides/README.md). **The front door** at [clickemt.com](https://clickemt.com/) is the base's signed-out screen: the join code first (it opens the field app with the code filled in, the link a join card's QR carries), its footer says which version is on screen with a Reload; a missing address gets `404.html`, the same join-code door with the ways back; a new version reloads that screen by itself once the worker takes over, while a signed-in base or phone gets a "New version ready" chip instead; then one picture of the base and a crew's phone, how it works in three steps with the real screens (`images/front/`), the six roles as doors into their tours, five promises an agency can hold us to, Pilot it with your agency, and the sign-in for Operations last. **Try it** at [clickemt.com/try](https://clickemt.com/try/): a public demo that runs the base and the phone side by side on the in-memory stand-in (`?demo`), no account and nothing sent anywhere, with the base and the phone each on a page of their own too (`/try/base/`, `/try/phone/`, Full screen and Reset; open both and they share the incident), and with a tour for each of the six roles that sell (`try/tours.js`): a story in steps, **Show me** rings the button in the frame and **Do it for me** presses it, so a visitor on a phone can watch the loop happen; the other roles get their guide.

## Stack

Plain JavaScript ES modules, no build step. Leaflet with OpenStreetMap tiles. Supabase (Postgres, Realtime, Storage) in the shared charity-tooling project, where every table carries the `ic_` prefix. Hosted as a static site; the field app is a PWA.

## Live

The repo is `clickconstruction/clickEMT` on GitHub, published with GitHub Pages from `main`:

- Base: https://clickemt.com/
- Field: https://clickemt.com/field/ (open it on the phone and add it to the home screen)

The custom domain is set by the CNAME file at the repo root; the older clickconstruction.com/clickEMT/ and github.io addresses redirect here.

A push to `main` deploys; bump `VERSION` in `sw.js` first when a shell file changed.

## Run it

Any static server works. From the repo root:

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8766 --bind 127.0.0.1
```

Then open `http://localhost:8766/` for the base and `http://localhost:8766/field/` for the field app. The camera, GPS and compass need HTTPS on a phone; localhost counts, a plain `http://` address on the LAN does not. The deployed site (GitHub Pages) is HTTPS.

### Mock mode: no account, no network

Add `?mock` to either address on localhost (`http://localhost:8766/?mock`, `http://localhost:8766/field/?mock`) and `dev/mock-supabase.js` stands in for Supabase: an in-memory copy of the tables, the triggers that write the log, the row-level security rules, Realtime and the photo bucket, seeded with one incident, Cedar Fork Flooding: six zones around a river town, three units with positions, three photos with headings, a hazard and a morning of log lines. The base tab is signed in as Operations and the field tab as Unit 3, both open on the incident, and the field tab gets a pretend GPS that stands in A-2 and drifts a few metres at a time. Open both in the same browser and what one does shows on the other within a second: mark the zone clear on the field tab and it turns green on the base.

The data lives in the tabs' memory. A tab that opens while another mock tab is open copies that tab's data, so to start over close every mock tab, or run `window.__mock.reset()` in the console and reload the incident. `window.__mock.tables` is the data, `window.__mock.ids` the seeded ids, `window.__mock.geo` the pretend phone's position (set `drift: false` to hold it still). Sign out and sign in with any email address or mobile number: the stand-in shows the code it "sent" under the form (and on the console); a seeded address (`ops@mock.local`, `unit1@mock.local` to `unit3@mock.local`) is that person, anything else is someone new, whose account is made with the first code and who can check in with the code `CEDAR7`.

**Smoke test:** open `http://localhost:8766/?mock` in a window at least 1100 px wide, with no other mock tab open, and run this in the browser console:

```js
const { run } = await import('/dev/smoke.js'); await run()
```

It drives the base through the page (select a zone, set a priority, assign a unit, draw a zone and the boundary on the map, open a photo, acknowledge an alert, add a note) and the field through a second copy of the data module signed in as Unit 3 (a photo with a heading, an address clear, the zone clear with its marking, a hazard, an alert and standing down), and after each step checks what the base sees and what went into the log. It takes one and a half to five minutes at v69, depending on the machine (longer in a hidden Browser pane, which throttles timers) and returns `{ passed, total, failed }`. `run({ only: ['fieldReports'] })` runs one suite; the suites are `seeded`, `baseActions`, `boundary`, `fieldReports`, `emergency`, `messages`, `accountability`, `people`, `periods`, `assignment`, `search`, `facilities`, `reports`, `agencies`, `publicInfo`, `replay`, `log`, `layers`, `zoneCard`, `join`, `identity`, `legend`, `sections`, `ics100`, `walkfix`, `evacuees`, `members`, `safetyset`, `parbyname`, `checkIn`, `permissions`, `rewalks`, `transports`, `tags`, `invite`, `around`, `demo`, `newcomer`, `pages` (v64, the pages as windows), `credentials` (v69: a band claimed from a signed-out phone, licences submitted, confirmed and refused, the chips and the 211) and `passwordless` (v73: the base's landing and the phone's door by a code to an email or a mobile number, a wrong code, Resend, a link's landing). `demo` (v32) checks that `?demo` loads the stand-in and that `/try/` frames both views on it. `walkfix` (v25) checks the fixes from the persona walkthroughs (`docs/walkthroughs.md`) as each persona met them, with the field app at 375 px in a frame beside the base; `evacuees` (v26) drives a crew's house record, a shelter desk's People here and the base's shelter card the same way. `members` (v27), `safetyset` (v28) and `parbyname` (v29) do the same for the Members tab and the clearing, the water clock, the settings and the public set, and the PAR by name.

## First run

1. Sign in on the base: your email or mobile number, then the six-digit code it sends (or the link in the email); a new address makes its account on the first code (sign-ups must be on and, for text messages, an SMS provider set on the Supabase project; see docs/handoff.md).
2. Open a new incident. It gets a six-letter join code, shown on the base's home panel.
3. On a phone, open the field app, sign in, and check in with the code and a unit name.
4. On the base, draw a zone and assign it to the unit. The phone shows the assignment; what the phone sends shows on the base.
