# 1. Dana Ruiz, Incident Commander

**Who you are.** The small-town fire chief at the command post, pulled in five directions. You need to know in thirty seconds whether anyone is in trouble, how far the search has got and what the plan is; to sign the Incident Action Plan; to brief the shift change; and to name a unified command when the county arrives.

**Where you work.** The base, on the laptop at the command post. Everything below is on the base unless it says Phone.

**In the mock.** The base tab is already signed in as Operations on Cedar Fork Flooding. Your seat is on the Period sheet: press **Period** in the header and read the Incident Commander line.

## Your jobs

### 1. Thirty seconds: is anyone in trouble, how far has the search got, what is the plan

- **Base** · Read the header: the period and its times, who is IC, how many units are on scene, how many divisions and how many are cleared.
- **Base** · Read the bar under the header. It is red (EMERGENCY, with Acknowledge and Show unit) while a unit needs help, yellow (SAFETY, PAR or MISSING) while a message, a head count or a missing person is open, and says "No emergency open" otherwise.
- **Base** · The legend under the map counts divisions by status: Not started, In progress, Primary search done, Cleared. Press a status to light only those divisions.
- **Base** · Press **Briefing** for the whole picture on one sheet: seats, agency representatives, the safety message, objectives, divisions, resources, who is in the water.

### 2. Sign the IAP

- **Base** · Press **IAP**. It is stitched from the data: the 202 (objectives), the 203 (organization), the 207 (chart), every division's 204 with its map and medical lines, the 205 (communications), the 206 (medical) and the 208 (safety).
- **Base** · **Print** it or **Copy as text**. There is no signature line on screen; sign the paper.

### 3. Brief the shift change

- **Base** · Press **Briefing**, then **Print** or **Copy as text**. The relief reads it as it stands; nothing has to be written first.
- **Base** · To start the next period, press **Period** and then **Start period N+1**. Open objectives and the seats carry forward.

### 4. The county is here: name a unified command

- **Base** · Press **Period**. Fill the **Unified command** box beside the seats, and the **Agency representatives** line for each agency on scene (one name and phone per agency, for the Liaison Officer). Both print on the 203 and read in the briefing.

### 5. Hand command to the county chief

- **Base** · Press **Period** and type the new name in the **Incident Commander** seat. The app asks "Transfer command from you to them?" with three boxes: tell everyone in the field, open the briefing for the handover, start a new operational period now.
- **Base** · Press **Transfer command**. Every phone gets a card to acknowledge, the handover briefing opens, the 214 gets a notable line, and the 203 keeps who held command when. **Just change the name** corrects a typo without any of that.

### 6. Who is in which seat, and is anyone stretched

- **Base** · Press **Org chart** for the ICS 207: the seats, the branches and divisions with their supervisors, the units and their people, with the span of control counted on every box. Past seven a box turns orange and the briefing says so ("Operations is at 1 : 8").
- **Base** · Press **Period** to seat a **Deputy Incident Commander** when a span is past seven (the seat prints on the 203 and sits under you on the 207), and to seat the Safety Officer, the Public Information Officer, the Liaison Officer and the four section chiefs. An empty seat prints as "not set".

### 7. What changed while I was away

- **Base** · The log's "N new" badge counts lines since you last looked. Press the star on a line to mark it Notable; the Notable chip keeps those, and the printed 214 leads with them.
- **Base** · Press **Replay** and choose "the last hour" to watch the units and photos move.

## The app does this for you

- Every report (briefing, sitrep, IAP, 203, 207) is assembled from the data, so it is current the moment you open it.
- Units acknowledge a transfer of command on their phones; the base shows who has not yet.

## Rough edges

- There is no "since you last looked" summary; the log's new-lines badge and Replay are the nearest.
- The IAP has no Prepared by / Approved by line.
- The transfer notice on a phone is headed like a safety message.
