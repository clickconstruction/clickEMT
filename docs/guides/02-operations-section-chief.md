# 2. Marcus Bell, Operations Section Chief

**Who you are.** The base map's main user, in front of it for twelve hours. You draw divisions and set priorities, assign and reassign units, see photos as they arrive, keep the head count honest, answer the radio with the map in front of you, and never lose a report.

**Where you work.** The base. Nearly everything on it is yours.

**In the mock.** The base tab is signed in as Operations with six divisions, three units and a morning of log lines. The phone tab is Unit 3, in A-2, so what you send reaches a phone you can see.

## Your jobs

### 1. Draw a division and give it a priority

- **Base** · Press **Draw division**, click the corners on the map, press **Finish** (Backspace takes the last corner off). The New division form opens in the sidebar: code, structures, name, priority. Press **Save division**.
- **Base** · **Draw boundary** draws the incident boundary the same way.
- **Base** · Change a priority on the division's panel, or right-click a division on the map or in the list for its card: rank, priority, unit, progress, hazards, photos, last activity.
- **Base** · Give the division its words on the panel: work assignment, special instructions, radio channel, supervisor, a branch word. They print on the 204 and show on the unit's phone.

### 2. Assign a unit, move it later

- **Base** · Open the division (the list under Divisions by priority, or the map) and press a unit's name under **Assigned to**. Pressing a name adds the unit; a unit may be on more than one division at once.
- **Base** · To move a unit, release it from the old division and press it on the new one. The unit's phone shows the new assignment with Accept.
- **Base** · Set a unit's status on its panel: Assigned, Available, Out of service, or checked out.

### 3. See the photos as they come in

- **Base** · A photo is a pin on the map and a log line with View photo. Its panel shows which way the camera faced ("Facing E 095°"), who took it, when, where, the unit's note, and whether it is cleared for the public.
- **Base** · **Cleared for public information** is your call; the PIO captions and checks it afterwards.

### 4. Run a PAR, and see who has not answered

- **Base** · Under Accountability press **Start PAR**. The yellow bar reads "N of M answered · waiting on …". The "PAR" chip under Message the field is only a message; the head count is Start PAR.
- **Base** · A unit with people on record answers by name. A missing person shows as "MISSING · Patel (Unit 2) · 14:31" on the bar, red on the People board and on the unit's panel with the unit's last position, and raises an emergency.

### 5. Answer the radio

- **Base** · Click a unit's marker (U1) for its panel: assigned to, standing in, position, heading, last report, head count, people with hours on scene, requests, Set status, Message.
- **Base** · Open a division for its status and progress. Type an address in **Find in the log** to see whether it was cleared and by whom.

### 6. Hand the map to your relief

- **Base** · Press **Briefing** and print or copy it. Press **Period** then **Start period N+1** to carry open objectives forward.
- **Base** · **Pop out** puts the log alone in its own window for a second screen.

### Also yours

- **Base** · **Message the field**: one unit, a division's unit, or everyone. Tick Safety to take over every phone until acknowledged. Each phone answers Copy, Will do or Cannot.
- **Base** · **Requests** from the field (hands, a boat, an ambulance, fuel, supplies) go asked, approved, ordered, filled, or cannot be filled, with a word back the unit sees.
- **Base** · **Facilities**: place the command post, staging, shelters, hospitals, boat ramps, helibases with a contact each. The phone shows the nearest with directions.
- **Base** · **Layers**: streets, satellite, Google Maps when a key is set, and your own aerial imagery laid over the map by its corner coordinates.
- **Base** · **Print bands** (v55), on the join box and the People board: a sheet of 24 wristbands, a QR and a number each and no name. The People board and the unit panel read each person's band and last scan ("Band 001 · seen entering A-1 07:05", orange when older than two hours); **Void band** on a row retires a lost one; the 211 lists them; the PAR panel says how many each unit scanned.
- **Base** · **Transports** on the header is the board (v54): **New run** plans a bus, a van, an ambulance or a boat run from a pickup to a shelter or a hospital, with its seats, its times and who drives; each row has one button for the next step (Loading, Leaving, Arrived, Done), Edit, Withdraw and who is on it. A late run says so. On the shelter's card the runs **Coming here** replace the typed Expected line, and each arriving family's row has **On which run**. The situation report counts runs under way and people in transit.
- **Base** · The home panel's Incident section ends with **Close incident** when it is over; **Reopen incident** undoes that.

## Rough edges

- Pressing a unit under Assigned to adds it; it never moves it. That is by decision.
- The briefing is the handover; nothing carries what you had open to your relief.
