What it is made of migration 0035_credentials.sql, the next free; a v55-sized release

A person with an account on a roster

ic_people.claimed_by: the person's own account. A crew member on their own phone is a person on the roster, not a second unit: no member row, no position, no PAR. user_id (the unit they ride with) becomes nullable for "not on a unit yet". One live row per account per incident.

ic_is_member gains the roster: a claimant reads the incident like a member, so every policy follows from the one function (ic_sees_profile gets the same clause, so Operations sees a claimant's identity). The phone's crew mode is a branch like the desk's: the You card, the unit's assignment and the briefing; messages and positions stay the unit's in this release.

The claim

ic_claim_band(code, name, phone, agency, unit), security definer: finds the band by its code on an active incident; refuses a voided band, a band on someone, and a caller who already has a row here. Saves the name and the phone to the profile (so ic_profile_ready holds), takes over the unit's unclaimed row of the same name when the lead typed it, else makes one; gives the band (ic_tag_before accepts the person's own account) and writes the check-in scan. Quiet underneath, then one line: "Rosa Alvarez claimed Band 025 (Unit 2)".

Credentials belong to the account

ic_credentials: one row per card, per account, never per incident. Kind from one fixed list (CREDENTIAL_KINDS in rules.js = the SQL check): driver, emt, paramedic, swift_water, boat, rope, k9, hazmat, drone, other. A number, an issuer, an expiry, a photo in the private ic-ids bucket under the owner's folder (the bucket's policies already cover it). Status pending · confirmed · rejected with a reason, who decided and in which incident. Archived, never deleted.

Triggers. ic_credential_before: the owner inserts pending only; a changed number, issuer, expiry or photo goes back to pending (as a profile change clears Mark verified); the status moves only through ic_decide_credential; a rejected one keeps its reason. ic_credential_after writes the 214: submitted (to every active incident the owner is on), confirmed, not accepted. The kind's words are ic_credential_kind_text in SQL and credentialKindText in rules.js, the same.

Policies. Read: the owner, and Operations of an incident the owner is on. Insert: the owner. Update: the owner's own fields; Operations only through the function. No delete.

The ID stays the profile

The first row of step 3 writes ic_profiles as today's sheet does (number, state, expiry, the front's photo). "Checked" is verified_at, set by Mark verified or the Members tab's Check. One record for who you are; the credentials are what you can do.

The 30-day clearing

ic_clear_personal also blanks a credential's number and photo (the photo into ic_id_purge) for the same people, and keeps the fact: kind, issuer, expiry, status, who decided and when. "Swift water, confirmed by Operations 18 Oct 2026" survives; the card's number does not.

How a chip reads

credState(person, credentials) in rules.js: for a claimed person, each qualification kind looks for a credential of that kind: confirmed → "Swift water ✓", pending → "EMT · pending", rejected → "Boat ✗" with the reason in the title, none → the plain word the lead tapped. A confirmed credential the lead did not tap is added. The 211, the board, the hover card and the phone read the one function. The driver's licence is never a chip.

Decided here, and yours to decide

Decided here (say if any is wrong)
  • Credentials belong to the account. They follow the person to the next flood and the next county; Capt. Reyes's "typed in each time" ends. An incident only decides and logs.
  • A claim puts the person on the incident at once; approval is asynchronous. The check-in table never queues behind Operations. The chip says pending until someone at the base has a quiet minute; the phone says so too.
  • One fixed list of kinds, shared by both views and the database. Chips, counts and the 211 need the same words; a free list cannot be counted. Driver's licence first, as the ID; Other last, with a title.
  • Nothing is deleted. A rejected credential stays with its reason and the person may submit a new one; a withdrawn one is archived. The 30-day clearing blanks the number and the photo and keeps the fact.
  • The ID stays the profile. The licence row writes ic_profiles as today's sheet does; "checked" is Mark verified. One record for who you are, separate from what you can do.
  • A crew member's phone is a roster row, not a unit. No position dot, no PAR, no second marker for Unit 2. The You card, the unit's assignment and the briefing are what it shows; the lead's phone stays the unit's.
Yours to decide
  • Is a band alone a way in? Its code carries the join code's first three letters, not the code. Drawn here: the band alone claims (a). The risk: a found band lets a stranger onto the roster under a name they typed, until Operations removes them; the band is on them and the 214 shows it. The other ways: the band plus the six-letter code, one more box on the sheet (b); or the band only when the lead already typed their name (c), which keeps the table's words "give me your crew's names". The build differs by one check in ic_claim_band.
  • Who confirms. Operations of the incident now, from the queue (drawn here); the agency administrator before the season, which needs the admin screen persona 11 still lacks; or both, with "confirmed by Travis County Rescue" counting so Operations need not look. The queue and the chip words are the same either way; the decider's role changes the policy.
  • Is a driver's licence the ID, or does an agency card count the same? Drawn here: the licence. An agency card would be a kind with its photo, and "ID given" would read from either.
  • Do confirmed credentials outlive the 30-day clearing as facts? Drawn here: yes (the kind, the issuer, the decision; never the number or the photo). The other way wipes the row to its kind alone. Also whether the clock follows the owner's last incident or each one.
  • What the lead sees when a crew member claims a band on their unit. Drawn here: the count on the lead's unit card goes up and a toast says "Rosa Alvarez joined Unit 2 by band". The other ways: a 214 line only; or the lead must accept, one more tap, and the person waits, which cuts against decision two.
  • How much of the incident a crew member's phone shows. Drawn here: the You card, the unit's assignment and the briefing. Messages, the PAR and positions could follow in a later release.
