The card is the breakout. Six rows, one per kind of thing, each with its facts whole and a button to where it is worked.
  • Operational period: started, planned to, and the alarm in words; Start period 2 right there when it is overdue, since that is the one action the line has ever asked for.
  • Command: the IC and the two chiefs the line could not fit; Org chart.
  • Base: the name and the lead agency.
  • Units: the count by status, then each unit, its kind and its division; Units scrolls the home panel there, People opens the board.
  • Divisions: the count, then a chip per state that filters the map as the legend's buttons do; the list itself is on the home panel, always.
  • Hazards: each open hazard in a line: kind, where, when, by whom.

Is this the best we can do? what the line is for, what was cut, what was set aside

What the line is for
  • It is read all day, in the corner of the eye, by someone watching the map. A status row, not a sentence: the alarm first, the counts next, nothing that stays the same.
  • Three questions it must answer at a glance: is the period overdue, how many units are out, how much is left to clear. Each is now its own short piece.
  • Everything else is a hover away and a click to keep.
What was cut from the line, and why it is safe
  • 19:58 to 19:00: the planned hours matter twice a day, at the start and the hand-over; the card and the Period panel have them.
  • IC Dana Ruiz: the IC changes by a transfer of command, which tells every unit, opens the briefing and writes a notable line (v24); nobody learns it from the header.
  • Base: Cedar Fork High School: set once at the opening; the join card and the briefing carry it to the people who need it.
  • OP becomes Period: plainer, and the button beside it says the same word.
Set aside
  • A chip per fact in the header, each opening its own panel: a second row of controls under thirteen buttons, the "too much going on" the walk at v59 named; the card gives the same links in one place.
  • A real modal (a dialog) on hover: a hover must never trap, a dialog needs a close, and the map stops being watched while it is up.
  • Hover only: a touch screen has no hover, and a card that goes when the pointer moves cannot be clicked into. Click keeps it.
  • Keep the line as it is and add the card: the card does not fix the line; the note was about the line.
  • Wrap the line onto two rows: more room for the same compaction.
What to watch after
  • Whether anyone misses the IC's name on screen. If they do, it goes back as the last piece, after the counts, not the second.
  • Whether the card wants the join code and the connection too: today those have their own cards under their pills (v60), and three cards in one header is enough.
  • At the base's phone width the line wraps to two rows and the card takes the width under it; nothing else changes.

Decided here, and yours to decide

Decided here (say if any is wrong)
  • A card, not a dialog. The codes' and the connection's cards under the header's pills (v60) are the pattern; this one hangs under the line at the left. chosen
  • Rest half a second or click; click keeps it. The division card's rule for the rest (v60), the connection card's for the click; Escape and a click elsewhere close it. chosen
  • One rule writes both. The line's pieces and the card's rows come from one function in rules.js, so they never disagree, and a Node test reads them. chosen
  • No migration. Everything on the card is already in the period, the incident, the members, the zones and the hazards. chosen
Yours to decide (recommended first)
  • What the line keeps. Recommended: the period, the alarm or the planned end, the units, the divisions and cleared, the hazards; the times, the IC and the base live on the card. The other way keeps every fact on the line and only adds the card. open
  • Overdue in orange, second. Recommended: yes, the one alarm reads as one; the other way leaves the line all grey as today. open
  • Start period 2 on the card. Recommended: yes, when overdue only: the line's one ask gets its one button, and the Period panel opens on the hand-over as it does from its own button; the other way keeps the card to facts and links. open
  • The divisions as chips that filter the map. Recommended: yes, they are the legend's buttons with the counts on them; the other way is plain words. open
  • "Period 1" or "OP 1". Recommended: Period 1 on the line and "Operational period 1" on the card, the vocabulary the base already uses (v4's list); OP stays on the printed forms, where ICS wants it. open
  • The card's width. Recommended: 560 px, so each row is one line at 1280 wide; the other way matches the codes' card at 400 and wraps the units. open

What a build would touch a v60-sized panel: no migration, the base only, one release

  • rules.js: incidentLine({ period, units, zones, hazards }, now) returns the line's pieces (words and kind) and incidentCard(…) the rows; both tested in dev/test-rules.mjs.
  • index.html: #inc-sub becomes a button, .inc-line, with aria-expanded; #inc-pop beside it, a .pop like #codes-pop.
  • ui.js: incidentPopHtml(data, state) draws the rows; the chips carry data-action="legend-status" so the map's filter is the same code.
  • main.js: drawAll writes the line from the rule; a 500 ms rest timer like the division card's; state.incOpen for the click; Escape and the click-elsewhere rule the codes' card uses; period-next from the card.
  • base.css: .inc-line, .inc-pop, .ic-row, the chips; the phone-width rule.
  • The suites: seeded and periods read #inc-sub for "overdue" today and would read the line and the card instead; one new check for the rest, the click and Escape.
  • Docs: a README line, a CLAUDE.md gotcha, the punchlist's section, "Built as vN" here, the re-walk; the discover labels (v58) say "the incident's name and its state" and keep saying it.
