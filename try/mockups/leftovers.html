The walk's leftovers: four small things from 18 October

Todd walked the base at v59 on 18 October and most of what he marked shipped that day and the next (v60 to v76). Four small things were seen on the way and never decided; docs/punchlist.md section 15 carries them. Drawn 19 October at v76, with incident kinds, the centre by address and the 214's water words on their branches; the header and the fresh incident built as v80, the rest to follow. One section per item, live where it can be, then the thinking, the decisions with the recommended answer first, and the builds. Made-up data; press things.

  1. One setting, the clock's click
  • Today's click swaps the clock alone (ic.base.clock, per device, like the theme). The cut: the same click swaps every time on this screen. There is never a UTC clock over a local log, which was v59's reason to keep the log local.
  • Every UTC time carries a Z: 00:36Z. A local time stays bare, as ICS has it; the zone's short name sits on the clock and on the forms' head.
  • The toast on the click says it in a line: "Every time on this screen is UTC now" and back.
  • The date follows the time: at 19:36 CDT it is 20 October in UTC, so the 214's Prepared line, the log's day line and "yesterday" all move with it.
How the printed forms say which it is
  • One line in every form's head, in both modes: Times local, CDT or Times UTC (Z). Two or three words that settle the question a mutual-aid reader has.
  • The Prepared line reads the same clock: "Prepared by Operations, 20 Oct 2026 00:44Z".
  • The printed 214's hour lines and every line's time swap with the screen; Copy as text carries the same words.
What stays local, and why
  • The phone. A crew reads the time on its wrist and its radio; the base's choice is a device setting and never reaches a phone. A Zulu agency's crews would get the same switch in the phone's own settings, later, if asked.
  • The date and time boxes (a period's planned end, a picture's Taken at, Replay's Pick a time): the browser's picker is local by nature; their label says "local" while UTC is on.
  • The Hours CSV already writes UTC and says so in its column names (checked_in_utc); the Hours page follows the screen.
  • "5 min ago", "4h 8m", "overdue 36m": durations have no zone.
  • The public photo set: its captions are for the public, who read local time.

Is this the best we can do? what each is for, what was set aside

The header: what the row is for
  • Nine doors, pressed all day by someone watching the map. The cost that matters is presses, not pixels: the labelled row adds none, the menus add one to the two most used.
  • Label 2 of Show me around already explains the row in these four groups; the row should look the way the label reads.
  • Set aside: icons in place of words (a map app's icons are learned; a base is opened twice a year); a second row of buttons (the walk at v59 ended one); the forms moved into the Period panel (the IAP is the period's, the 207 and 209 are not); a row that changes shape with the window's width on its own (a row you cannot learn).
The fresh incident: what the first minute is for
  • One person, one screen, nothing on it yet. Three things to do in order: the boundary, the divisions, the phones in. The screen should say the next one and nothing else.
  • The labels (v58) say what each part is; the parts say what they lack. Two sets of words that never repeat each other.
  • Set aside: a checklist or wizard ("2 of 3 done": a sheet over a map that already says it); a sample division to delete (nothing is deleted); hiding the log (its one line is the incident's first); labels with fresh-incident words (two sets of label words to keep in step); the codes in the panel for good (v60 put them behind the pill so they are never on a screen someone walks past; here they show only while no phone is in).
UTC: what the switch is for
  • A mutual-aid agency on Zulu time, or a base that spans a zone line. Everyone else never presses it. So it costs nothing to those who do not, and the whole screen to those who do.
  • The dangerous state is a mixed screen. One setting that moves everything is the fix; two settings bring the arithmetic back.
  • Set aside: both times on every line ("19:36 / 00:36Z": twice the digits for everyone); a per-form switch (the clerk prints six forms); UTC on the phones by the base's choice (a crew's wrist is local); a Z on local times (ICS forms are local and bare); a time zone picker (the device's zone is the local one; UTC is the one other that matters).
The pictures: what they are for
  • A visitor's first look, before Try it. Each should show the thing the words beside it say, at the base as it is.
  • A picture is stale the week after a screen changes; the fix is a script that makes redoing them cheap, more than the pictures themselves.
  • Set aside: a dark set beside the light one (the landing is light); an animated run (a picture loads first); rendering the base wider than 1280 (a laptop's width is what a reader has); the Layers panel open in the hero (under the phone).
What to watch after
  • Whether the labels over the row read as headings of something below them, on the first open; label 2 names them, which should settle it.
  • Whether the first Operations misses Messages and the PAR before the first unit; both appear with it, and the briefing says so.
  • A UTC log crossing midnight: the day line above the hour line is the one new thing in the log; the same rule gives a local log its date past midnight, which it never had.
  • The pictures: re-shot once more when the starters land, since the New incident sheet is not on any of them and the header may gain the kind.
