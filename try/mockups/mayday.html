Need help now: what's wrong, what you need

Todd, 18 October: on this screen we should be able to add better context, either what kind of help or the nature of the danger. Drawn at v62, before anything is built. Send alert stays one tap, exactly as today. Two rows of big chips above it, optional. The words ride on the alert to the bar and the 214, and the chips stay live after the send, because the first press is a reflex and the detail comes a breath later. Made-up data; press the buttons.

The sheet, at 375 px one shared state: Send alert and every chip on the two live phones reach the base below

The story is at 17:45 Unit 2 (Dep. Moreno, alone at the bridge post, B-1) pressed bare at 17:43 for a car in the water. Unit 3 (Hale, Dominguez, A-2) is about to send: a trailer shifted onto Hale.

No typing. The words are in the list's order, never the tap's, so the base reads them the same way every time.

The base, the same moment the bar, the unit panel and the 214 follow the phones above; Acknowledge reaches the phone

The alarm line, one cell per open emergency, cut short on screen and whole on hover. Today's words first, then the chips' words, then the time, so a bare press reads exactly as today and the words survive the cut before the clock does. The buttons stay Acknowledge and Show unit.
The unit panel. The red badge carries the words. Nothing chosen reads "needs help" as today.
The 214, written by the database's trigger, never by the app: the alert with its words after the colon, the acknowledgement, each chip added or taken back after the send, the stand-down. Filed under the field as today's help lines are.

What it is made of one migration, the next free number (0035 today; 0036 if the credentials build takes 0035 first); a small release

The lists live in rules.js

ALERT_WRONG and ALERT_NEEDS: code and label pairs like URGENCY, in the order the chips are drawn and the words are read. alertWords(a) joins them: the wrong words lower case with commas, then " · needs " and the needs, then the PAR's note when there is one ("in the water · needs boat"; "trapped, injured · needs boat, medic"; "Missing: Okafor"). The phone's chips, the bar, the unit panel and the mock read the one function; dev/test-rules.mjs covers it. In SQL, ic_alert_text(wrong, needs, note) says the same words, as ic_qual_text does for qualifications.

Two columns, not the note

ic_alerts.wrong text[] and needs text[], empty by default, each checked against its list. The codes travel; the words are read. note stays what v29 made it, the PAR's "Missing: Okafor", and is read after the chips. Why not the note alone: the line after a send has to say what was added, and a trigger can take the difference of two arrays in one expression; taking it out of a joined string, around a PAR's words, is a parser nobody wants in SQL and in the mock twice. The cheaper build (the words into note, no new columns) is the first thing in Yours to decide.

The policies are enough

Checked: ic_alerts_update (0001) lets the unit update its own row and Operations any (standDown already goes through it). No new policy. One new before trigger, ic_alert_before: the chips change only by the alert's own unit, never once stood_down_at is set; Operations keeps acknowledged_at. The insert policy is unchanged (ic_may_write on the queued time, v27).

The trigger writes the line

ic_log_alert (0021's body, the highest that defines it) gains one branch between the two it has: when wrong or needs changed, "Unit 2 adds: in the water · needs boat" from the codes now present and not before, and "Unit 2 takes back: rope" from the ones gone. The insert line reads ic_alert_text in place of note, so a bare press still writes "EMERGENCY, Unit 3 needs help in A-2" and the PAR's alert still ends ": Missing: Okafor". One tap is one update, so a line never says both; two taps queued offline arrive in order and write two lines.

The phone, the base, the outbox

  • The phone: state.alertWrong / alertNeeds (sets), cleared by openHelp; the chips drawn by one function on both sheets; sendAlert puts the codes on the row and the outbox entry; a tap on Help sent writes the local row and queues kind alertWords {id, wrong, needs} → data.setAlertWords, a plain update like standDown. A tap while the alert is stood down does nothing (the screen is gone). The PAR's missing report sends its note as today, no chips.
  • The base: maydayHtml appends " · " + alertWords(a) after the zone; unitHtml's badge the same; no second chime for a change of words, the cell's words change in place. The attention line in the briefing reads them too.
  • The mock: DEFAULTS gets the two empty arrays, afterInsert and afterUpdate the two lines with the same words, the check on the codes; the seed gets one stood-down alert from the morning with words, so the log shows the form. SEED_VERSION bumps.
  • Rule test supabase/tests/alertwords.sql, rolled back: a bare insert writes today's line exactly; two chips write the words after the colon; a code outside the list is refused; the unit adds one and the "adds" line lands, takes one back and the "takes back" line lands; another unit's update is refused; Operations cannot change the chips; a change after stand-down is refused; the PAR's note still lands with no chips.
  • Smoke suite mayday, the phone in phoneFrame as the walkfix suite does: opens Need help now, counts ten chips, sends bare and reads the bar as today; taps Medic on Help sent, reads "needs medic" on the bar and "Unit 3 adds: medic" in the log; stands down; opens again, taps two, sends, reads the words in the first line and on the unit panel's badge.
  • Size: a v41-sized release (hazards with an urgency): one migration, rules.js, both views, the mock, a rule test, a smoke suite. A day or two.

Decided here, and yours to decide

Decided here (say if any is wrong)
  • Send alert does not change. Same button, same place, one tap, no chip required. The chips sit between the card and the foot, where the thumb is, and the foot stays stuck to the bottom however long the sheet is (v42).
  • Two rows, both optional, both multi-select. What's wrong tells Operations what they are walking into; what you need tells them what to send. Either row alone leaves a question: "in the water" with no need could be a body or a swimmer. Ten chips, five rows of two, 52 px each: a glove hits one.
  • The lists changed from the first recommendation. "Person in the water" and "Vehicle in water" became one chip, In the water: the car is a detail, the need chip (Boat) says what matters, and the position says where. Lost became Cut off: in a flood a crew is cut off by rising water far more often than lost, and the phone's position already tells the base where a lost crew is. Someone missing is new: a lead whose crew member was swept away presses this button first, not a PAR. What you need stays Boat · Medic · Rope · More hands: a mayday is not a 213RR.
  • The chips stay live on Help sent. The first press is a reflex. A tap after the send updates the open alert and writes its own line; a tap on a lit chip takes it back, logged, because gloves slip. The base's cell changes in place with no second chime.
  • No free-text box. Typing in the rain with one hand is what the alert exists to avoid. What the chips cannot say goes by radio, or by Message Operations, which has the box and the pin already.
  • The words are read in the list's order. "trapped, injured" whoever tapped which first, so the bar reads the same way every time and the smoke suite can read it.
  • Codes in two columns; the note stays the PAR's. One migration either way (the after-send line needs a trigger branch), and arrays make the "adds" line one expression. The sentence on the sheet is shorter: "Goes to Operations and every unit, with your position."
Yours to decide
  • The cheaper build. The words joined into note, no new columns: the insert line works today with no SQL, and only the after-send line needs the trigger. The cost is parsing the note to say what was added, and a PAR's "Missing: Okafor" mixed into the same string. Drawn here: the two columns.
  • Chips on the sheet, or only after the send? Drawn here: both, the same chips in the same place. The other way keeps the sheet exactly as today and shows the chips only on Help sent: the alert lands a second sooner for everyone, and Dep. Moreno's car in the water takes one more tap after Send. Either way the base's bar carries the words.
  • "Injured" or "Hurt". Injured is the radio word and leaves out a heart attack; Hurt is plainer and leaves it out too. "Medical" covers both and is the word nobody says. Drawn here: Injured.
  • Does an added "In the water" chime again at the base? Drawn here: no, the cell's words change and the 214 line lands. The other way rings once more for a change of words on an unacknowledged alert only.
  • A chip for "Tools" (jacks, cutters, cribbing)? Trapped under a shifted trailer needs them. Drawn here: not yet; four needs keep the row to two lines. Add it when a walk asks for it.
  • Operations setting the chips from the base. Drawn here: the unit's alone (the trigger refuses). The other way lets the base add "needs medic" after a radio call, with a line that names Operations.
