A · Facts first, one menu per row

The row says who, how long on scene and where they were last seen. Check out stays on the row; everything else is under ···. Made-up data; press the buttons.
For Operations at the map and the Safety Officer walking in. Three columns read straight down: a person past the period's hours or not scanned in two hours is orange, nothing else is. What it costs: five actions sit behind a mark a newcomer has to try once (hover says what it holds).

B · Tick and act

One quiet table. Tick a name, or a whole unit, and the actions appear above it for all of them. "Longest on scene first" puts the overdue at the top. Made-up data; press the buttons.
For the clerk and the shift change. Checking out a unit of six is two clicks; moving three people to a new unit is four. What it costs: nothing on the row says what can be done until something is ticked, and a wrong tick acts on the wrong people (nothing is lost: Back in and Put back undo it).

C · Cards and a drawer

A unit is a card, a person is a line on it. Click the line: everything about that person, every action spelled out, and their lines from the 214, in a drawer beside the cards. Made-up data; press the buttons.
For the first-timer at the post, and whoever asks "what happened to Patel?". The cards read like the units on the map; the drawer answers one person fully. What it costs: Check out is a second click, and the cards squeeze to two columns while the drawer is open.

Decided here, and yours to decide

Decided here (say if any is wrong)
  • The same three facts in the same order in all three: name (rank, qualifications), on scene, last seen. The band's number is part of "last seen", not a column of its own.
  • Orange is "look at this": past the period's hours on scene, or a band not scanned in two hours. Red stays for MISSING.
  • Check out stays a plain button in A and on B's bar: it is the press at the end of a shift, with gloves, and the one that frees a unit.
  • A move is never a submenu. The ··· menu and the drawer list the units by name, so a move is two clicks.
  • Remove and Void say "kept on record" where they are pressed, in five words; a removed person stays on the list, greyed, with Put back. The mock skips the confirm the app asks today.
  • The bar is unchanged: the find box, the qualification chips, Print bands, Print 211, Hours. The Members tab and Add a person stay where they are.
Recommended
  • A, with one borrowing from B: a "Check out the unit" on a unit's header for the shift change. A keeps every action on the row where a first-timer looks, at one or two clicks; the three facts read straight down; and it is the smallest build, since the row keeps its actions and the two dropdowns become menu items. B is a second way of working to learn, and its table is best for the one day a season when many people move at once. C reads best and answers "what happened to Patel?", but it costs a third of the width and a click on Check out; its drawer could come to A later as "Details" on the menu.
