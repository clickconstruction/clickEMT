Incident kinds and starter periods

The note, 19 October, as the main session relayed it: Offer buttons when making a new period: the user selects natural hazards, human-caused, medical and public health, search and rescue, planned events, and then sees options that pre-fill the period. They have not run an incident and want it easy to configure. Drawn at v76, before anything was built; built as v77 (docs/punchlist.md, section 36; the re-walk in docs/walkthroughs.md), every decision as recommended, with one change from the build notes below: no migration, the kind kept in the incident's settings until a column exists; the centre by address built as v78 (section 37). One piece: two rows of buttons on the New incident sheet, the kind of incident and then its usual sub-kinds, asked once; pressing a sub-kind gives period 1 a starter, a handful of short lines on the Period sheet that Operations can edit or remove with one press. Flood is written in full; the other sixteen are sketched in one line each. Made-up data; press the buttons below and watch the Period sheet fill.

    The key
    • The one config.js already carries for the Google layers, window.GOOGLE_MAPS_KEY. It is public, so it must be restricted in Google Cloud to the HTTP referrers https://clickemt.com/* and http://localhost:8766/*, with Places API (New) enabled beside Map Tiles API. Geocoding API was enabled too, and refused: Google does not let a key restricted to a site call it, so every call is Places (New).
    • The Incident settings key (gear) cannot serve this sheet: no incident exists yet when it is typed.
    • The settings' Test button (testGoogleKey in layers.js) opens a tile session today; it would also ask Places one word and say whether Places answers.
    • Todd makes the key himself, with the Maps tooling already used on clicktooling.com. Until it is in config.js the sheet is today's.
    What goes to Google, and what never will
    • The typed words go to Google as they are typed. For an incident's centre and a facility that is fine: a school, a bridge, a church.
    • That is also why no person's address will ever go through it: an evacuee's street, a licence's address and a house record stay typed, as today. The box is on two forms and no other.
    • Nothing on this page calls Google; the list here is nine made-up Cedar Fork places. The mock (dev/mock-supabase.js) gets a stand-in for the lookup so the smoke suite can type, pick and read the numbers.
    • The list says "powered by Google", which Google asks for when its suggestions are shown off a Google map.
    The calls, and the cost
    • places:autocomplete (Places API (New), REST, called with fetch and the key) with the typed words, a session token and a locationBias circle around the device's position; then places/{id} with fields=location,displayName for the pick, which ends the session.
    • Enter with nothing picked sends the words to Places Text Search (places:searchText, one result) and the first place sets the centre: a full address pasted in works too. The Geocoding API was the first draw; it refuses a referrer-restricted key.
    • Billed per session token: one per address typed and picked. A handful of incidents a year is cents.
    • No migration: the centre is center_lat and center_lng, as today; the base's name is the Base box.

    Is this the best we can do? what the sheet is for, what a starter is, what was set aside

    Who it is for
    • Someone at a laptop at 19:58 who has never opened an incident, with phones already arriving. The first screen after Create should say what to do tonight, in their own app's words, not in a manual.
    • Two presses, nothing to read first. The kinds are the note's words; the sub-kinds are the ones a county sees.
    • Someone who has run a hundred incidents loses nothing: both rows are optional, and an unpressed sheet is today's.
    What a starter is, and is not
    • A starter is a few short lines in the boxes the sheet already has: objectives, the safety message, the rotation and the hours, what to bring. Nothing new to learn, nothing new to store but the kind.
    • Twelve words at most on a line, two or three short sentences in the safety message: it is read on a phone in the rain, from the briefing.
    • The lines are Operations' own the moment they appear: the same tick, the same edit, the same × as a line they typed. A starter that is wrong is five presses from gone.
    • Place first and Units to expect are words from the table, not records: a press places a facility; a unit kind is what to ask for on a 213RR.
    Set aside
    • A wizard, steps with Next: the sheet is one screen and Create is its one button; a first-timer with phones arriving does not want five screens.
    • Templates per period: Start period N+1 already carries the seats, the open objectives, the rotation, the hours and bring; a template applied again would overwrite what the day taught.
    • Long canned text: a page of a safety message is not read on a phone and is not Todd's twelve words; the starter's lines are short, and the sheet keeps its boxes short.
    • Locking anything: a line that cannot be removed is a rule, and the app's rules live in rules.js and the database, not in a starter.
    • A kind on the header's line: v76 keeps the line to what changes; the kind never does.
    • A starter that places facilities on the map: four letters on one made-up spot, to be dragged apart by someone who has never dragged one. The sheet names them and each is a press to place (decision 3).
    What to watch after
    • Whether the third standing objective reads as noise on a parade or a game; it is one press to remove, and the planned-event rows could leave it out.
    • Whether seventeen sub-kinds is too many to scan. The groups fold them to four or five at a time; a county that only ever floods presses the same two buttons every time.
    • The words in the sixteen sketches: a crew of each kind has not read its row. Flood's words are the seed's, written at v4 and walked since.
    • Whether the first screen after Create should be the Period sheet (as drawn) or the home panel with the Period button lit.

    Decided here, and yours to decide

    Decided here (say if any is wrong)
    • On the New incident sheet, asked once. The kind is a fact about the incident, like its name and its base; periods carry forward by Start period N+1. chosen
    • Two rows, the kind then its sub-kinds, both optional. A kind alone records the word and starts period 1 with the three standing objectives; a sub-kind adds its own lines; nothing pressed is today's sheet. chosen
    • A starter is rows in one table in rules.js (STARTERS), read by both views, tested in Node for the twelve-word rule; nothing per period, nothing locked, no template anywhere. chosen
    • The lines are written on open, as Operations' own. Create makes the incident and period 1 as today, then writes the starter into period 1 (the notes, the rotation, the hours, bring) and inserts the objectives; the 214 reads "Incident opened: Cedar Fork Flooding, a flood, operational period 1 from the Flood starter" and then one "Objective N set" line each, as if typed. chosen
    • × on an objective removes it, never deletes it. Nothing the user made is deleted (the repository's rule), so a removed objective is archived (ic_objectives.archived_at), out of every list and carried nowhere, with a 214 line "Objective 5 removed: …". This also gives a mistyped objective a way out it never had. A cleared box is blank; a cleared rotation or hours is 0. chosen
    • Place first and Units to expect are words, not records. The chips come from the table for the incident's kind and each goes once a facility of its kind is on the map; the unit kinds are a line to read. Nothing written. chosen
    • Create opens the Period sheet when a starter was used, so the lines are the first thing seen; with no starter, the home panel as today. chosen
    • The kind can change in Incident settings. It changes the word on the card, the picker and the briefing; no period is rewritten. chosen
